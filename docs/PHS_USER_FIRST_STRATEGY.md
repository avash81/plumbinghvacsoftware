# PHS User-First & Human Connection Strategy

The starting question for every page is: **does this genuinely help the person reading it, and can they reach a real human if they need to?** This is achieved through tone, real authorship, and one simple contact page — not through adding new pages, tools, or features. Keep the site simple; make the simple parts genuinely human.

---

## 1. Make the Existing Contact Page Do This Job

No new "Ask Us" feature, no chat widget, no separate form. The single `/contact/` page already planned in the site structure covers this:

- A real, monitored email address, stated plainly, with a real response-time commitment (e.g. "We personally read and reply within 2 business days").
- One line inviting specific questions: *"Not sure which option fits your situation? Email us — we'll give you a straight answer."* This is copy, not a new feature.
- No chatbot pretending to be a person. If automation is ever used, it says so clearly.

---

## 2. Real Authors, Not Decoration

The `/authors/[slug]/` pages already in the plan (Guide Section 4.5) should show a real name, real background, and a real way to reach that specific person — not a stock photo or "Staff Writer." This alone does most of the work of feeling human, with zero new pages added.

---

## 3. Real Operator Voices Inside Existing Content

No new content type needed — just source material for the guides/reviews already planned:

- A handful of short conversations (phone/email) with real plumbing/HVAC owners per quarter, quoted by name and business where permitted, folded directly into relevant existing articles.
- Real specificity beats generic polish — concrete numbers and real operator language, not manufactured authority.

---

## 4. Write and Structure Every Page Around the Reader's Actual Decision

Before publishing, check a page against three questions:

1. What decision is this person trying to make?
2. What do they need to know to make it confidently — not everything knowable, only what's decision-relevant?
3. Is the next step obvious (a specific comparison link, or the contact page if their situation doesn't fit)?

If a section doesn't serve one of these, cut it — same discipline as the "no promotional language" tone rule, applied to structure.

---

## 5. Reduce Friction Instead of Adding Anything

- No sign-up wall on any content.
- No pop-ups before the reader has gotten value.
- Answer the real question near the top of the page, plainly.
- The existing contact page is the one, obvious place for anything that doesn't fit — no need for a second channel.

---

## 6. What This Actually Changes in the Plan

- No new routes, pages, or tools are added to the site structure.
- `/authors/[slug]/` and `/contact/` — both already planned — carry the full weight of "human connection." They just need to be written and built like a real person is behind them, not treated as boilerplate.
- One line added to the Content Writer Checklist (`PHS_SEO_Execution_Toolkit.md`): **"Would a real business owner reading this feel like a real person made it for them?"**

---

## 7. The One-Sentence Test

Before publishing anything, ask: **"If the reader knew a real human made this specifically to help them, would they trust it more or less?"** If less, rewrite it.
